Malaysia in 3.2 million dots
    Loading...

    In 2020 the census counted 32.4 million people in Malaysia. Here they are, one dot for every ten people.

    That is 3.24 million dots, each placed in the state constituency where those people live. They are modelled positions, not addresses.

    Scroll to read on. The buttons at the top jump straight to any part.

    1 · The country in dots

    Half of everyone lives on about 2% of the land

    The bright band runs down the west coast of the peninsula, from Alor Setar through Penang, Ipoh and Kuala Lumpur to Johor Bahru. Inland, the dots thin out into forest and plantation.

    The Klang Valley alone holds 28% of the people

    Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya together: about 9 million people on 2.5% of the land. The core stays solid because it really is that dense; zoom in at the edges and single dots appear.

    Point at any area for its census numbers.

    Sabah and Sarawak: 60% of the land, 18% of the people

    Across the South China Sea, people follow the coast and the big rivers. Kuching, Sibu, Miri, Kota Kinabalu, Sandakan and Tawau light up; the interior is mostly dark.

    2 · Who lives where

    Six groups, coloured

    Malay 52%, Chinese 21%, Other Bumiputera 11%, Indian 6%, Other citizens under 1%, and Non-citizens 8% of everyone counted.

    Malay dots fill most of the peninsula's north and east coast; Other Bumiputera dominate Sabah and Sarawak; Chinese and Indian dots gather in the west coast towns.

    Click a group in the list above to show it alone.

    In the Klang Valley the colours mix

    No group is far from any other here, but the mix still changes from one constituency to the next.

    Within each constituency the groups are scattered at random among its dots, so look at the mix of areas, not of streets.

    About one in four people in Sabah is a non-citizen

    Non-citizens are 2.7 million people, 8% of everyone, but about 24% of Sabah's 3.4 million. The census counts everyone living in the country, so they are part of every total on this page.

    3 · The dots leave the map

    Line everyone up by age

    Men to the left, women to the right, youngest at the bottom. The widest bands are people aged 25 to 34, about 2.9 million in each five-year band.

    Children under 15 are 24% of everyone; people aged 65 and over are 6.7%. The base is narrower than the middle: fewer babies are born now than a generation ago.

    Three in four live in urban areas

    24.4 million people in urban areas, 8.1 million in rural ones. Urban or rural comes from district counts, so it is modelled for each dot.

    4 · Income, district by district

    First, a guess

    Each district has a median household income: half its households earn less. On the card to the right, draw how you think the 160 medians rise from the poorest district to the richest, then press Show me.

    The real curve, and every district as a dot, appear after your guess.

    5 · Where do you stand?

    Put your own household on the chart

    Pick a district and type a monthly household income. The card shows how many households in that state earn less, as 100 dots of 1% each.

    Percentiles are published for states, not districts, so the comparison is with the whole state.

    6 · Explore

    Your turn

    Filter the dots by group, sex, urban or rural, and age, and find any district by name. The count tells you how many people are left on the map.

    Try the oldest age bands alone, or rural women.

    About this page

    • People and places: Census 2020 by state constituency and district, Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), open data on GitHub (dosm-malaysia/data-open).
    • Income: Household Income Survey 2022, district medians and state percentiles, OpenDOSM.
    • Where inside each area: Kontur population grid (400 m hexagons, 2023) sets where dots fall within each constituency.
    • Method: 1 dot = 10 people. Group, sex, age and urban or rural are drawn from the published tables for each area and mixed at random among its dots. Dots show patterns across areas, never individual homes.
    • Basemap © CARTO, © OpenStreetMap contributors. Built with MapLibre and deck.gl.
